## THREAD

**1/** <!-- ~215 chars -->
Ask people to picture the world after AGI and most describe the same thing: problems finally over. Disease cured. Scarcity solved. Work optional.

I don't think that's the best world. I'm not even sure it's a good one.

**2/** <!-- ~205 chars -->
The hidden assumption is that problems are a bug. Something we're trying to exit, like a loading screen before the real thing starts.

But problems aren't the loading screen. They're the game.

**3/** <!-- ~245 chars -->
David Deutsch: problems are inevitable, and problems are soluble. Both halves matter, together.

Every problem we solve creates new problems we couldn't have seen before. Better ones. Stranger ones. Ones we only reach because we climbed high enough.

**4/** <!-- ~220 chars -->
Agriculture solved hunger and gave us the problems of cities. The internet solved distance and gave us the problems of attention.

You solve one and you don't reach the end. You reach a new frontier of things worth solving.

**5/** <!-- ~210 chars -->
So an AGI utopia wouldn't be a world without problems. If it's any good, it's a world with harder, stranger, more worthwhile ones.

A world where nothing is left to solve is a world where nothing is left to mean.

**6/** <!-- ~250 chars -->
Meaning lives in problems. Not in solving them. In having the right ones.

It's working on something genuinely yours: hard enough to stretch you, real enough to matter, close enough to what you care about that solving it would actually change something.

**7/** <!-- ~230 chars -->
AGI makes this sharper, not softer. When the cost of solving almost anything falls toward zero, the whole game becomes choosing what to point that power at.

The scarce resource stops being the answer and starts being the question.

**8/** <!-- ~240 chars -->
We're trained to solve. We're barely trained to see: to find the real problem under the mess, stated well enough to work on.

In an age of machine solving, choosing the right problem might be the most human thing left to do.

**9/** <!-- ~200 chars -->
I don't want the utopia where the problems are over. I want the one where we've earned better problems than the ones we have now.
